## 📝 Course Description

This course introduces the principles and applications of geophysical methods used to investigate Earth's near-surface and subsurface geology. The course emphasizes the physical properties of Earth materials, geophysical survey design, data acquisition and processing, and geological interpretation. Topics include seismic refraction and reflection, ground-penetrating radar, electrical resistivity, nuclear magnetic resonance, self-potential, electromagnetic induction, gravity, and magnetic methods. Lectures are complemented by laboratory exercises involving field measurements, calculations, and interpretation of geophysical datasets.

The course is designed primarily for senior undergraduate and graduate geology students. It emphasizes using geophysical observations to answer geological questions and understanding the strengths, limitations, and uncertainties of each method.

---

## 🎯 Course Objectives

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

- Explain the physical principles behind common applied geophysical methods.
- Describe how seismic velocity, electrical resistivity, dielectric permittivity, density, and magnetic properties relate to subsurface geology.
- Identify appropriate geophysical methods for problems involving bedrock depth, stratigraphy, groundwater, fractures, faults, and buried features.
- Perform introductory calculations using travel times, wave velocities, electrical measurements, and geophysical anomalies.
- Describe fundamental survey configurations, data acquisition, and processing steps.
- Interpret common geophysical plots, profiles, and cross sections in a geological context.
- Recognize uncertainty, resolution limits, and nonuniqueness in subsurface interpretations.
- Communicate results clearly through laboratory exercises and short technical explanations.
